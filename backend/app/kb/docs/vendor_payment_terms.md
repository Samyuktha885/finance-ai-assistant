# Vendor and supplier payment terms

Payment terms with vendors and suppliers directly affect a business's cash flow
and working capital needs — negotiating favorable terms is a genuine financial
management lever, not just an administrative detail.

## Common payment term structures

- **Net 30 / Net 60 / Net 90**: payment due within that many days of the
  invoice date. Longer terms give the business more time to sell inventory or
  collect from its own customers before paying the supplier.
- **2/10 Net 30**: a 2% discount is offered if paid within 10 days, otherwise
  full payment is due within 30 days — a common early-payment incentive
  structure.
- **Cash on Delivery (COD)**: payment due immediately upon delivery, common for
  new vendor relationships before trust/credit history is established.
- **Advance payment / deposit**: partial or full payment required before goods
  or services are delivered, often for custom orders or new client
  relationships.

## Why negotiating terms matters for cash flow

Longer payment terms with suppliers effectively give a business free short-term
financing — cash stays in the business longer before being paid out. This can
meaningfully reduce working capital needs, especially for businesses with long
sales cycles.

## Building supplier relationships that support better terms

- **Payment reliability**: consistently paying on time (or early) builds trust
  that can lead suppliers to offer longer terms or better pricing over time.
- **Volume and relationship length**: established, high-volume relationships
  often unlock better terms than one-off or new vendor relationships.
- **Explicit negotiation**: payment terms are often more negotiable than
  businesses assume, especially for businesses that pay reliably — it's worth
  asking rather than accepting default terms.

## Balancing early-payment discounts against cash flow needs

Taking a 2% early-payment discount is effectively earning a high annualized
return on that cash (2% for paying 20 days early works out to a meaningfully
high annualized rate) — but only makes sense if the business has the cash
available without straining its own working capital elsewhere. This is general
education on vendor payment term structures, not specific negotiation advice for
any particular business relationship.
