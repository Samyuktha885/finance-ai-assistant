# Profit margin basics

Profit margin measures how much of a business's revenue actually turns into
profit, expressed as a percentage — a key indicator of financial health beyond
just looking at raw revenue or profit numbers.

## Three common margin measures

- **Gross margin**: (Revenue − Cost of Goods Sold) / Revenue. Shows profitability
  of the core product/service before accounting for overhead like rent and
  administrative salaries.
- **Operating margin**: (Revenue − Operating Expenses, including COGS) /
  Revenue. Shows profitability from core business operations, before interest
  and taxes.
- **Net profit margin**: Net Profit / Revenue. The bottom-line figure after ALL
  expenses, including interest and taxes — the most complete profitability
  picture.

## Why looking at margin percentage matters more than raw profit numbers

A business generating ₹10 lakh profit on ₹1 crore revenue (10% margin) is in a
very different position than one generating ₹10 lakh profit on ₹5 crore revenue
(2% margin) — the first has much more cushion to absorb cost increases or a
sales dip without becoming unprofitable. Comparing margin percentage over time,
and against industry benchmarks, reveals trends that raw profit figures alone
can hide.

## What causes margins to shrink

- Rising input/material costs without a corresponding price increase.
- Increased competition forcing lower prices.
- Operational inefficiencies (waste, overstaffing relative to output).
- Discounting too aggressively to win sales volume.

## Improving margins (general strategies, not specific advice)

- **Raising prices**: even small price increases can meaningfully improve
  margin if demand isn't highly price-sensitive.
- **Reducing COGS**: negotiating better supplier terms, reducing waste,
  improving production efficiency.
- **Reducing overhead**: cutting or renegotiating fixed operating costs.
- **Shifting product/service mix**: focusing more on higher-margin offerings
  relative to lower-margin ones.

This is general education on profit margin concepts — actual strategies for
improving margin should be evaluated against the specific business's
competitive position, customer expectations, and cost structure.
