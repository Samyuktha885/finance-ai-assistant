# Types of business loans

Businesses commonly borrow for different purposes, and lenders offer distinct
loan products for each.

## Working capital loans

Short-term financing to cover day-to-day operational expenses (inventory,
payroll, rent) during periods when cash flow is tight — not meant for long-term
asset purchases. Often has a shorter repayment period than other business loans.

## Term loans

A lump sum borrowed for a specific purpose (equipment, expansion, renovation)
repaid over a fixed period with regular installments (similar structure to a
personal loan, but usually with different eligibility criteria and interest
rates set for business borrowers).

## Line of credit / Overdraft facility

A flexible, revolving credit limit a business can draw from as needed and repay,
similar to a credit card but usually with a higher limit and lower interest
rate. Interest is typically charged only on the amount actually drawn, not the
full limit — useful for managing unpredictable short-term cash flow gaps.

## Equipment financing

A loan specifically for purchasing equipment or machinery, where the equipment
itself often serves as collateral, which can make this type of loan easier to
qualify for than an unsecured term loan.

## Invoice financing / factoring

Allows a business to borrow against outstanding customer invoices rather than
waiting for customers to pay — useful for businesses with long payment cycles
from clients (common in B2B services).

## Choosing between loan types (general education, not advice)

- Short-term operational gap: working capital loan or line of credit.
- Large one-time purchase: term loan or equipment financing.
- Waiting on client payments: invoice financing.

The right choice depends on the specific cash flow need, repayment ability, and
what collateral (if any) is available — a lender or financial advisor should be
consulted for a specific business's situation.
