# Break-even analysis for a business

Break-even analysis identifies the point at which total revenue equals total
costs — beyond this point, the business starts generating profit; below it, the
business operates at a loss.

## Key inputs needed

- **Fixed costs**: expenses that don't change with sales volume (rent, salaries,
  insurance) over a given period.
- **Variable cost per unit**: the cost to produce/deliver one additional unit of
  the product or service (materials, direct labor, packaging).
- **Selling price per unit**: the price charged to the customer per unit.

## The break-even formula

Break-even point (in units) = Fixed Costs / (Selling Price per Unit − Variable
Cost per Unit)

The denominator (selling price minus variable cost) is called the **contribution
margin per unit** — it represents how much each unit sold contributes toward
covering fixed costs before any profit is made.

## Why this matters for business decisions

- **Pricing decisions**: understanding break-even helps evaluate whether a
  proposed price point is realistic given cost structure and expected sales
  volume.
- **Cost-cutting priorities**: since fixed costs directly raise the break-even
  point, reducing fixed costs (rather than just variable costs) often has a
  bigger impact on how quickly a business becomes profitable.
- **Growth planning**: knowing the break-even point helps set realistic sales
  targets when planning expansion, new product lines, or entering new markets.

## A note on break-even in revenue terms

Break-even can also be expressed in revenue (rupees) rather than units, useful
when a business sells multiple products at different prices:

Break-even revenue = Fixed Costs / Contribution Margin Ratio

where Contribution Margin Ratio = Contribution Margin per Unit / Selling Price
per Unit.

This is a foundational business finance concept — actual break-even
calculations should use a business's real, current cost and pricing data, not
estimates, for meaningful accuracy.
