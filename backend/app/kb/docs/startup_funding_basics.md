# Startup and business funding: debt vs equity

When a business needs capital to grow, there are two fundamentally different
ways to raise it, each with very different implications.

## Debt financing

Borrowing money that must be repaid, typically with interest, regardless of
whether the business succeeds or struggles.

- **Pros**: the business owner retains full ownership and control; interest
  payments may be tax-deductible; once repaid, there's no ongoing obligation.
- **Cons**: must be repaid on a fixed schedule regardless of business
  performance, which creates risk during slow periods; typically requires
  collateral or a strong credit/revenue history to qualify.
- Common forms: business loans, lines of credit, invoice financing (see
  business_loan_types.md).

## Equity financing

Raising money by selling a percentage of ownership in the business to
investors, in exchange for capital.

- **Pros**: no fixed repayment obligation — investors share in the business's
  risk, not just its upside; can bring investor expertise/network beyond just
  capital.
- **Cons**: gives up a percentage of ownership and future profits permanently;
  investors typically expect some level of input into business decisions;
  raising equity capital (especially from institutional investors) can be a
  lengthy, complex process.
- Common sources: angel investors, venture capital, friends and family
  investment, crowdfunding platforms.

## How businesses typically decide between the two

- **Predictable cash flow, want to retain full ownership**: debt is often
  preferred, since it can be serviced from predictable revenue without giving
  up equity.
- **High-growth, uncertain near-term profitability** (common for early-stage
  startups without steady revenue yet): equity is often necessary, since the
  business may not have the cash flow to service debt repayments reliably.
- **Many growing businesses use a mix of both** over time, adjusting the balance
  as the business matures and cash flow becomes more predictable.

This is a foundational overview of financing types — the right mix for any
specific business depends on its stage, industry, growth plans, and risk
tolerance, and is a decision typically made with input from a financial advisor
or accountant.
