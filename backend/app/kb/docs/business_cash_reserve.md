# Building a business cash reserve

A business cash reserve (sometimes called a rainy-day fund or contingency fund)
is money set aside specifically to cover unexpected disruptions — a slow sales
month, an unexpected major expense, a delayed customer payment, or a broader
economic downturn — without needing to take on emergency debt or halt
operations.

## How much to reserve

A commonly cited guideline is **3 to 6 months of fixed operating expenses**
(rent, salaries, loan payments, insurance — the costs that continue regardless
of sales volume). Businesses with more seasonal or unpredictable revenue often
aim for the higher end of that range, or beyond.

## Why this differs from working capital

Working capital covers the *normal* operating cycle (paying for inventory
before collecting from customers). A cash reserve is separate — it's a buffer
specifically for *abnormal* disruptions, not day-to-day operations. Confusing
the two can leave a business under-protected against genuine emergencies.

## Where to keep a business cash reserve

Similar principles to a personal emergency fund apply — prioritize
accessibility and safety over growth:

- **Business savings account**: fully liquid, low yield.
- **Short-term, liquid instruments**: some businesses use short-term liquid
  funds or short-tenure fixed deposits with early-withdrawal flexibility for a
  portion of the reserve, balancing some yield against needing quick access.

Avoid tying reserve funds up in illiquid investments or long-term assets — the
entire purpose is that the money is available in full, quickly, when an
unexpected need arises.

## Building the reserve over time

Many businesses build this gradually by treating a fixed reserve contribution
as a non-negotiable line item in monthly budgeting, similar to any other
recurring expense, rather than only setting aside money if there happens to be
a surplus at the end of the month. This is general guidance on cash reserve
planning — the appropriate target amount depends on the specific business's
revenue stability, industry, and risk factors.
