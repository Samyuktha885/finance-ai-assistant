# Cash flow management for small businesses

Cash flow is the movement of money in and out of a business — distinct from
profit, since a profitable business can still run out of cash if payments are
timed poorly (e.g. paying suppliers before customers pay invoices).

## Why cash flow matters more than profit for survival

A business can be profitable on paper (revenue exceeds expenses over a year) and
still fail because cash isn't available *when* bills are due. This mismatch —
income and expenses happening on different timelines — is one of the most common
reasons small businesses run into trouble, even growing ones.

## Key components to track

- **Cash inflows**: customer payments, loan disbursements, owner investments.
- **Cash outflows**: supplier payments, rent, salaries, loan repayments, taxes.
- **Cash flow gap**: the period between paying for goods/services and receiving
  payment from customers — the longer this gap, the more working capital a
  business needs to bridge it.

## Common ways businesses improve cash flow

- Negotiating longer payment terms with suppliers (paying later) while
  encouraging customers to pay sooner (e.g. early payment discounts).
- Maintaining a cash reserve specifically to cover the gap during slow periods.
- Using short-term financing (like a line of credit) to bridge temporary gaps,
  rather than as ongoing debt.
- Reviewing recurring expenses regularly for anything that can be trimmed
  without hurting operations.

## A simple monthly cash flow check

Comparing total cash in vs total cash out each month (not just revenue vs
expenses on paper) gives an early warning sign — a business can spot a cash
crunch coming weeks before it happens, rather than being surprised by it. This
is general education on cash flow concepts, not a substitute for a proper
accountant or financial advisor reviewing a specific business's numbers.
