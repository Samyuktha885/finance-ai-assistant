# Categorizing business expenses

Organizing expenses into clear categories is foundational to understanding
where a business's money actually goes, and is typically required for tax
filing and financial reporting regardless of business size.

## Common expense categories

- **Cost of Goods Sold (COGS)**: direct costs of producing goods/services sold
  (raw materials, direct labor for production).
- **Operating expenses**: rent, utilities, salaries (non-production staff),
  marketing, software subscriptions, office supplies.
- **Capital expenditures**: purchases of long-term assets (equipment, vehicles,
  property) — typically depreciated over time rather than expensed all at once.
- **Financial expenses**: loan interest, bank fees, payment processing fees.

## Why the COGS vs operating expense distinction matters

COGS scales directly with sales volume — more units sold generally means more
COGS. Operating expenses are more fixed (rent doesn't change based on how much
is sold in a given month). Separating these two categories makes it possible to
calculate **gross margin** (revenue minus COGS), which shows how profitable the
core product/service is before accounting for overhead.

## Fixed vs variable expenses

- **Fixed expenses**: don't change much with sales volume (rent, salaries,
  insurance premiums).
- **Variable expenses**: scale with sales volume (raw materials, sales
  commissions, shipping costs).

Understanding this split is essential for break-even analysis and for
understanding how profit margins change as sales volume grows or shrinks.

## Why consistent categorization matters over time

Using the same categories consistently month over month makes it possible to
spot trends (e.g. marketing spend creeping up faster than revenue) that would
be invisible if categories kept changing. This is general education on expense
categorization — actual tax-deductible categories vary by jurisdiction and
business type, and should be confirmed with an accountant.
