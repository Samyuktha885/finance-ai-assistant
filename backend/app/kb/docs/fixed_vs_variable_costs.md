# Fixed vs variable costs

Understanding which costs are fixed and which are variable is foundational to
pricing, break-even analysis, and profitability planning.

## Fixed costs

Costs that stay roughly the same regardless of sales volume, at least within a
given range of output:

- Rent or lease payments
- Salaried employee wages (not commission-based)
- Insurance premiums
- Loan payments
- Software subscriptions

These costs must be paid whether the business sells 10 units or 1,000 units in
a given month.

## Variable costs

Costs that scale directly with production or sales volume:

- Raw materials
- Direct labor tied to production output (hourly/piece-rate workers)
- Sales commissions
- Packaging and shipping costs
- Payment processing fees (often a percentage of each transaction)

## Semi-variable costs

Some costs have both a fixed and variable component — for example, a utility
bill might have a fixed base charge plus a variable amount based on usage, or a
sales role might have a fixed base salary plus commission.

## Why this distinction matters for decision-making

- **Break-even analysis** depends entirely on correctly separating fixed and
  variable costs (see break_even_analysis.md).
- **Scaling decisions**: a business with mostly variable costs can scale
  production up or down more easily without financial strain, while a business
  with mostly fixed costs needs consistent sales volume to stay profitable.
- **Cost-cutting strategy**: cutting variable costs affects unit economics
  directly; cutting fixed costs lowers the break-even point but may require
  structural changes (renegotiating leases, reducing salaried headcount).

## A simple way to think about operating leverage

Businesses with high fixed costs relative to variable costs are said to have
high "operating leverage" — profits grow faster once break-even is passed, but
losses also grow faster if sales fall short, compared to a business with mostly
variable costs. This is a foundational concept for understanding a business's
risk profile, not a specific recommendation for any individual business's cost
structure.
