# Business insurance basics

Business insurance protects a company against financial losses from specific
risks — separate from personal insurance, and often required by law, landlords,
or business partners depending on the type of business.

## Common types of business insurance

- **General liability insurance**: covers claims of bodily injury, property
  damage, or personal injury caused by the business's operations, products, or
  employees — often one of the most fundamental coverages for any business.
- **Property insurance**: covers physical business assets (equipment,
  inventory, the building itself if owned) against damage from fire, theft, or
  other covered events.
- **Professional liability insurance (Errors & Omissions)**: covers claims of
  negligence or mistakes in professional services provided — particularly
  relevant for consulting, advisory, or service-based businesses.
- **Business interruption insurance**: covers lost income if the business is
  temporarily unable to operate due to a covered event (e.g. fire damage
  forcing a temporary closure).
- **Key person insurance**: covers the financial impact on the business if a
  critical employee or founder becomes unable to work or passes away.
- **Workers' compensation**: covers medical expenses and lost wages for
  employees injured on the job — often legally required once a business has
  employees, though specific requirements vary by jurisdiction.

## How to think about which coverage is needed

The right combination depends on business type, industry risk factors, whether
the business has physical premises, whether it employs staff, and contractual
requirements from clients or landlords. A service-based consulting business has
very different insurance needs than a business handling physical inventory or
manufacturing.

## Insurance as risk management, not just compliance

Beyond legal requirements, business insurance is a tool for protecting the
business's continuity — a single uninsured lawsuit, fire, or major equipment
failure can be enough to end an otherwise healthy business. This is general
education on business insurance categories — specific coverage needs and policy
details should be discussed with a licensed insurance broker familiar with the
business's industry.
