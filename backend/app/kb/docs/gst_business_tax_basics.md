# GST and business tax basics (India) — conceptual overview

GST (Goods and Services Tax) is an indirect tax applied to the supply of goods
and services in India, replacing several older indirect taxes with a unified
system. Rules and rates change periodically — always verify current rates and
thresholds with official GST portal guidance or a tax professional rather than
relying on a fixed figure.

## Key concepts

- **GST registration**: businesses above a certain annual turnover threshold are
  required to register for GST and charge it on their sales. Thresholds differ
  by state and business type (goods vs services).
- **Input Tax Credit (ITC)**: businesses can typically claim credit for GST paid
  on their own business purchases, offsetting it against GST collected on
  sales — this avoids tax being charged multiple times on the same value chain.
- **GST returns**: registered businesses are required to file periodic returns
  (commonly monthly or quarterly depending on business size) reporting sales,
  purchases, and tax collected/paid.

## Why GST compliance matters for cash flow

GST collected from customers isn't the business's money — it's collected on
behalf of the government and must be remitted. Businesses sometimes make the
mistake of treating GST collected as available cash, which can create serious
cash flow problems when the payment is due. Setting aside GST collected in a
separate account (rather than mixing it with operating cash) is a common
practice to avoid this.

## Corporate/business income tax (general concept)

Separate from GST, businesses also pay tax on their profits, with different
structures depending on business type (sole proprietorship, partnership, private
limited company, LLP) — each has different tax treatment, rates, and compliance
requirements. This assistant does not have current-year specific tax rates or
thresholds and should not be relied upon for exact tax calculations — consult a
chartered accountant or the official tax authority for precise, current figures.
